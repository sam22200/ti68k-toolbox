# Alundra: reverse-engineering notes (movement, jump, terrain heights)

Study for `ti-port-ps1`: learn how Alundra's traversal works, then build a small TI-89
interpretation in one test room. Not a port. Labels: **OBSERVED** (measured or read in the
game), **INTERPRETATION** (what it likely means), **TARGET** (what we build).

## Decisions (grilling, 2026-10-04)

- Goal: one standalone test room (purpose-built, not an original room): move, jump, three
  height levels, ledges reachable and not, drops, walls, corners, narrow passages, readable
  depth. No combat, enemies, original assets or rooms. Stop there.
- Fidelity: the *feel* (jump timing and reach in seconds and tiles), the *ledge rules* and the
  *responsiveness*; trajectory and visual elevation only need to read well.
- Jump on a button only (no auto-hop); report if Alundra auto-hops.
- Heights: discrete, levels 0 to 2; 1 level = 8 screen pixels.
- A jump clears +1 level, never +2 (a +2 ledge acts as a wall); walking up +1 is blocked.
- Walking off any height: a fall at the jump's descent speed, no damage, nothing special.
- No slopes or stairs in the first room.
- 8 directions; steering in the air as in the game (measured below: full air control).
- Collision: a foot box smaller than a tile (about 10 × 8) against per-tile heights.
- Sizes: 16 × 16 tiles; the player 14 × 20 with a white outline (mock-ups A 12×16, C 14×20,
  B 16×22 compared; C chosen: Alundra is 1.25 × 1.75 tiles, 12×16 too small, 16×22 too big
  for 100 rows).
- One room on one screen (10 × 6 tiles) for milestones 2-5; a camera only if the room needs it.
- Controls: arrows, [2nd] jump, [diamond] debug overlay, ESC quits.
- Depth: rows drawn back to front, the player inserted at its row (Y then Z), 8 px front faces
  per level, a shadow on the ground under a jump; height numbers in the debug overlay.
- C on the Portable Game Runtime, PC first, TI last, grayscale, no asm.
- **Art from the disc** (user, 2026-10-04, after the grilling): not a 1:1 port, but the
  graphics, assets and sprites are taken from Alundra (tiles, the player's frames), converted
  to 4 greys and fitted to 16 × 16 tiles and the 14 × 20 player. Like MGS: generated by a build
  tool from the local disc, never committed. Source: the PS1 VRAM in play (`psxrun.py --vram`,
  1024 × 512 × 16 bit: framebuffers at x 0-319, character sprites as 4-bit textures from x 320,
  palettes in the bottom rows) and the GPU packets that draw them (texture page, CLUT, UV).
  Milestone 6: the player 20 px tall at the game's proportions first; then (user, 2026-10-04)
  **22 px tall** (size B: Alundra looked small next to the tiles), scale 22 / 42 = 0.524, ~12
  wide standing, the widest jump poses cut to the 16-px sprite. **Speeds = Alundra's in screen
  pixels scaled by the same 0.524** (the user found 3 px per step too fast: same tiles per
  second, but 1.4× faster than the game relative to the body, 2× in depth): 2.375 px per step
  across, 1.625 in depth (the game's 2/3), continuous in 1/16 px as before. The jump (apex
  13.9 px, 27.5 × 0.524 = 14.4) and a level (8 px, 16 × 0.524 = 8.4) already were at that scale.
  Shadow: always very visible (user): adaptive, see § Behaviour, Art.
- Milestone 8 (user, 2026-10-04): an approximation of the village of Inoa, its terraces
  compressed to 3 levels (Experiment 10): upper terrace 2, stairs 1, ground 0, walls; the
  retaining wall stays a 2-level wall, the stairs become two +1 jumps; the engine unchanged.
- Milestone 9 (user, 2026-10-04): the whole village of Inoa, no interiors, the exits closed, no
  NPCs; the scale a parameter (Makefile `SCALE`, default 22/42); the terraces compressed to two
  levels each (levels 0-15 now); real stairs (walked one level up or down); smooth scrolling
  (the TileMap engine on tiles baked from the game's own drawing); walls first as blocks, then
  textured by kind (stone, roof, tree). Experiment 11.
- Milestone 9, revised (user, 2026-10-05: "it must look like the village"): the game's own
  image of the village as the background, so **the true heights** again (a level = 16 px of
  the game, 8.375 screen px: the image and the collision agree), terraces two to four levels
  apart as in the game; stairs walked two levels per tile.
- Milestone 2 (2026-10-04): constant integer gravity in 1/16 px instead of a jump table: two
  additions per step like a table, and falls of any height come for free (Alundra's own
  model). Positions in 1/16 px (u16 x, y; s16 z, vz).

## Addresses

Objects: an array of 64 at `801ac6f8`, 0x294 bytes each; **the player is object 0**. Fields
(offsets from the object): +0x6c flags, +0x88 state, +0xe4/+0xe8 dx/dy this frame, +0xec vz,
+0x114/+0x118/+0x11c x/y/z (16.16), +0x138 floor, +0x140 grounded, +0x148 corner cell pointers
(x4), +0x158 corner floor heights (x4), +0x1d8..+0x1e0 box origin, +0x1e4/+0x1e8/+0x1ec box
offset (-10, -7, 0), +0x1f0/+0x1f4/+0x1f8 box size (21, 15, 32: 22 x 16 x 32 px).
Map: pointer at `801ac6c4` (0x80153e08 on the ship), cell (col, row) at
map + 0x604 + col*8 + row*0x1a0: 52 columns of **24 px** (x → col by the table at
`80137990`, one short per pixel) × rows of **16 px** (y >> 4), 8 bytes per cell:
[0..1] flags (0x0001 edge/wall: blocks like a wall, 0x0040 wall), [2] & 3 slope type
(0 flat, 1 ramp along y over 16 px, 2/3 ramps along x over 24 px from a 24-entry table at
`800236d4`), [3] height in units of 16 px.
Code: `FUN_8003797c` floor sampler, `FUN_80037eb8` vertical step, `FUN_80038008` horizontal
move resolver, `FUN_80037d4c` the player's wall test per corner, `FUN_800377e0` object boxes.
See `alundra.sym`. Two structs hold the player:
- `801ac7e0..`: the physics object (master): vz +0x04, x +0x2c, y +0x30, z +0x34 (16.16),
  floor +0x50, four sampled floors +0x70..+0x7c with four pointers +0x60..+0x6c (map cells?).
- `80133990..`: a display copy (x +0x10, y +0x14, z rounded +0x1c low, anim state +0x70,
  anim timer +0x7c counting 0x1e..1).

## Behaviour

**Logic rate.** OBSERVED: x changes every displayed frame. INTERPRETATION: the field logic runs
at 60 Hz. TARGET: our logic step at the TI frame rate (~30 fps) with every speed doubled per
step, or two steps per frame if the budget allows; decided at milestone 2.

**Planar movement.** OBSERVED: walking speed along x 0x27000 = 2.4375 px/frame (146 px/s);
along y 1.625 px/frame, exactly 2/3 of x; diagonals 1.7234 / 1.1489 (each axis × 0.707). The
first and the last frame of a walk move half a step (0x13800). In the air x speed is 0x24c00
= 2.30 px/frame. INTERPRETATION: the world is foreshortened in depth (map cells 24 × 16, the
same 3:2), speed normalised on an ellipse; no real acceleration. TARGET: constant speed, no
inertia; 16 × 16 tiles are not foreshortened, so y speed = x speed (the 2/3 would make
vertical moves feel sluggish on a square grid); diagonals × 0.75 per axis (a shift and add).

**Jump.** OBSERVED: CROSS sets vz = +5.0 px/frame; every frame z += vz then vz -= 0.5; apex
27.5 px after 10 frames, back on the floor after ~21 frames (0.35 s); the same arc standing or
walking. At rest vz stays at -0.5 (the floor holds z). INTERPRETATION: a classic constant
gravity jump, separate from the planar motion (x/y and z independent). TARGET: a jump table of
z per step (integer pixels), since the arc is fixed.

**Air control.** OBSERVED: during a jump, releasing the arrow stops x at once, the opposite
arrow reverses at full air speed the next frame; walls stop x during the jump without changing
the arc. INTERPRETATION: full air control, no momentum. TARGET: the same.

**Falling.** OBSERVED: dropped from above the floor, z follows the same gravity from vz = 0
(18 px in 8 frames); terminal speed not measured yet. TARGET: the jump table's descent.

**Terrain heights.** OBSERVED: the floor under the player is a value in world pixels, in
multiples of 16 (0x40, 0x50, 0x80, 0x90, 0xa0, 0xb0, 0xc0 on the ship), changing on an 8-pixel
grid in x and y (blocks of 24 to 48 px, not aligned to the 16-px graphic tiles); ramps
(stairs) change by ~5.5 px every 8 px. The player's z rests at the floor. INTERPRETATION: a
3D block map (per 8 × 8 cell: a height), the screen showing a block at y - z. TARGET: one
height per 16 × 16 tile, levels 0-2, 1 level = 8 screen px (decided).

**Floor under the player** (read in `FUN_8003797c`, 2026-10-04). OBSERVED: the floor is sampled
at the four bottom corners of the player's box (22 × 16 px) and **the highest of the four** is
the floor. INTERPRETATION: the player stays on a ledge while any part of the box is over it,
and drops only when the whole box is past the edge. TARGET: the same with a 10 × 8 foot box
(two to four corner samples against per-tile heights).

**Landing** (`FUN_80037eb8`). OBSERVED: when vz <= 0, if z + vz <= floor the player lands:
z = floor + 1/65536 (the low "0001" of z), grounded = 1; otherwise z += vz. While rising there
is no terrain ceiling (only other objects' boxes stop a jump). INTERPRETATION: land when the
arc crosses the floor; heads never hit terrain. TARGET: the same test per step on the table's z.

**Ledges and walls** (`FUN_80038008` + `FUN_80037d4c`, verified on synthetic ledges). OBSERVED:
a horizontal step is tried, the floor resampled; on the ground, a difference of less than
3 px with the current z is followed (z snaps to it: ramps and tiny steps); then a corner blocks
if its cell has a wall/edge flag (0x01, 0x40) or **its floor is at or above the player's feet
(z)**. A blocked step is retried with half steps, then slides along walls and around corners
(direction-dependent nudges of ±0.75 px). Verified: walking into +16 stops at the cell edge;
a jump into +16 passes once the feet are above it and lands on it; +32 blocks walk and jump
(apex 27.5 px); walking off a ledge drops with gravity; a lower cell flagged 0x01 (the ship's
side) blocks like a wall, also in the air. No auto-hop. INTERPRETATION: a ledge is a wall
until the feet are higher than its top, so the jump's apex decides what is reachable.
TARGET: tile heights in levels; a move into a tile blocks if the tile is a wall or its height
is >= the feet's z; on the ground z follows the tile (no ramps in our room); one level =
16 world px in Alundra < apex 27.5 < two levels: our jump table's apex must be between one and
two levels (in our units: > 8, < 16 px when 1 level = 8 screen px), so +1 is reachable, +2 not.
Corner sliding: yes, cheap version (try x alone, then y alone).

**Corners** (`FUN_80038008`, read in the code for milestone 5, verified by experiment 9). OBSERVED: the wall test
(`FUN_80037d4c`) returns one flag per box corner, in the order left-back, right-back,
left-front, right-front (back = smaller y). The resolver first halves a blocked step until it
fits (flush); only when **no part of the step fitted**, once per frame, it switches on the
facing direction (+0x8c & 31: 0 down, 8 left, 16 up, 24 right, the others diagonals):
- straight: if the two corners behind are free and **exactly one of the two front corners**
  is blocked, the move becomes a sideways nudge away from that corner: x ±0.75 px (0xc000),
  y ±0.5 px (0x8000) per frame, i.e. ~0.31 of the walk speed on both axes; both front corners
  blocked = stop (a flat wall);
- diagonal: the axis whose side corner is blocked is dropped and the step retried (slide);
  only the corner itself blocked = stop.
The same code runs on the ground and in the air. INTERPRETATION: corners are rounded whatever
the overlap (up to the whole box width minus one pixel), so a narrow passage or the side of an
obstacle "catches" the player and guides them in. TARGET: per-corner mask (`al_block`); a
straight move that makes no progress with one front corner blocked nudges 1 px per step
(NUDGE = 16, 1/3 of the walk speed) away from it; diagonals keep x then y.

**Walls.** OBSERVED: walls are either cells flagged 0x01/0x40 or cells too high; railings
and the ship's sides are flagged cells (lower than the deck but impassable).
TARGET: a wall flag per tile, plus "too high = wall".

**Depth and occlusion.** OBSERVED (synthetic blocks poked into the map, screenshots): the
scenery is drawn from the height map itself (poking cells changes the picture); a block 24 px
in front of the player (larger y) and 48 px high hides the player's body, only the head shows
above its top; at the apex of a jump the body shows above the block; stepping back hides more.
INTERPRETATION: screen y = y - z, blocks drawn back to front with the player inserted by
depth (y). TARGET: rows drawn back to front, the player drawn after its row and before the
rows in front, raised by z (decided: Y then Z sort, 8 px front faces per level, ground shadow).

**Art** (milestone 6, `tools/extract.py`). OBSERVED: the scenery is drawn as 24 × 16 sprites
(GP0 0x65, 4-bit, several CLUTs, in layers: the 24-px map cells); the characters are textured
quads (GP0 0x2C-0x2F) in two packet buffers (double-buffered, ~0x801c6000 and ~0x801cb000).
Alundra is two quads, body (24 × 32) above legs (24 × 24, drawn first, overlapping by 7 px),
texture page (320, 256) 4-bit, CLUT (192, 497); his whole sheet stays in VRAM (walk, jump,
attacks). Walk: 6 images per direction, 10 frames each at 60 Hz; the stop shows a standing
image per direction; a jump shows a take-off image for 1-2 frames then one air image; on a
diagonal the sprite faces up or down; left = right mirrored (u runs backwards); a shadow quad
(semi-transparent, page (448, 256)) under him at all times. TARGET: the poses read from the
packets every frame while the game plays them (not cut from the sheet by hand: the packets
give the part layout per pose), 0.48 scale, palette → 4 greys by hand, white outline.
**Placement and breathing** (milestone 6, measured from the packets): the side walk images
sway around the legs' centre (the body up to 4 px behind it on a stride), the front and back
ones stay centred; TARGET: keep each image's offset (`hero_ox`). Standing, after the last walk
image (2 frames), the game cycles stand 40 frames, breath in 10, out 4, in every direction;
TARGET 21, 5, 2 steps at 32 fps.
**Shadow** (measured on the deck, a standing jump): one quad 24 × 16 (page (448, 256), uv
(152, 88), CLUT (192, 480), GP0 0x2F semi-transparent), a filled ellipse, drawn at all times,
centred on the ground point; during a jump it stays on the floor and shrinks a little (22 × 14
at the 27-px apex). Its centre is 5 px above the bottom of the legs' quad (deck and village, both packet buffers).
TARGET (user, 2026-10-04: "the shadow is ultra important", replacing the
earlier "only in the air"): the disc's ellipse scaled by 0.48 (12 × 8; 11 × 7 when the feet are
a level or more above the floor), always drawn on the floor, its centre 2 px above the feet, as a semi-transparency:
every pixel under it one grey darker (white → light → dark → black, both planes in one pass),
so the floor's texture shows through. Then (user: "the shadow during a jump must always be very
visible"): **adaptive** — the greys under the mask are summed first (a 16-entry bit-count
table); on a light floor (mean < 1.5) every pixel goes two greys darker (white → dark grey,
light grey → black), on a dark floor it is a black ellipse with a light-grey ring. 13 × 8 and
12 × 7 at the 0.524 scale, centre 3 px above the feet.

   areas (the collision cell pointers at +0x60..+0x6c stay those of the old position).

   0x90 cells there are the top of the hull, a wall. The jump search's "0x90 positions" were

10. The village's terrain (2026-10-04), the map read from `inoa.state` (§ Addresses). OBSERVED:

11. The whole village (2026-10-04), `inoa.state`. OBSERVED: the map rows 11-57 are the village
   (rows above: height 0, nothing; below: other data), 52 columns of 24 px. Per TI tile
   (41 × 25 at 22/42): heights 1 (198 tiles), 3 (113), 7 (28), 10 (200), 13 (48) are the
   terraces; 11 (7 tiles, benches and crates on the 10 terrace); 15-18 and most slope cells
   above the terraces are **roofs**: a house is wall cells (flags 0x41) for its walls and
   slope or flat cells 5-18 for its roof, not flagged; slopes between two terraces are stairs.

   y − z − feet y), the feet read from the player's GPU packets, so screens taken over a
   grid of teleports paste into one image of the village in (x, y − z). INTERPRETATION: a

   camera has not moved for 3 frames (100 frames of settling). The NPCs: every object but the

   scenery (fountain, lamps) stays. The top rows of the map are seen only in the HUD's rows:
   the image is not reliable there (behind the closing walls).
