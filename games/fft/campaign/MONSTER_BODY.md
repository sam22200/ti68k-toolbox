# Wide monster bodies in battle (FMB1, `ffmb`)

2026-11-15. The battle renderer can draw monster bodies wider and taller than the
16x26 human actor: idle, walk, attack, hurt and Dead, front/back plus mirrors,
with the same cover, water and flash rules as the human sprite. The engine is
data-independent; it is tested with two synthetic test creatures generated by
`check_monster_body.py` (a round one, a 44-pixel wide one with claws). No game
art is in the repository or in this check. The bank's content comes from a
manifest of PNG poses packed by `tools/monster_body_bank.py`.

## Runtime

- `monster_body.h`: cold validator `monster_body_valid` (bounds, offsets,
  frame headers, no opaque bit under the transparency mask) and direct lookup
  `monster_body_frame(bank, job, anim, t, hold)`.
- `monster_body.inc`: `monster_body_active`, pose selection and
  `draw_monster_body`, called by `draw_unit_covered` after the FKO1 Dead path
  (an FKO1 pose, when present for the job, still wins).
- `chapter_load` borrows the optional `ffmb` archive in place (`resource_file`),
  like `ffko`. An invalid bank refuses the chapter. No allocation, no Work or
  State field, no ABI change.

Pose selection: `P_KO` → Dead, `P_HIT` → hurt, the attacker's wind-up/blow/throw
→ attack timed from `ACT_WIND` and held on its last frame, the walker → walk,
otherwise the idle loop staggered by `11*i` like the human idle. Kneel (Critical)
uses idle: the basic set has no kneel pose. Front/back comes from
`unit_direction` bit 0, the mirror from bit 1, around the feet column
(x ↔ 2·sx−1−x, as the 16-column human sprite).

Drawing is in 16-column strips straight from the archive (no actor-cache entry):
covered interior white, covered shadow (rows below the feet) transparent,
the depth-1 water crop at the human line with its light edge, hit/objective
flash on the interior except the shadow rows. A strip whose rows nothing
changes (no mirror, no flash, no water, no scenery in front) is handed to
`draw_sprite` straight from the archive on the 68000, as the human sprite is;
only the rows the scenery really bites are rebuilt, and the scenery in front
is read once per row for every strip at once rather than once per strip. The enemy diamond and the death
counter move above a body taller than a human (adaptation: one row above its
outline). Monsters never reach the human 16-column lookup: `actor_animate`
skips them, and a crystal/chest dissolve of a monster uses the object frame
only. The perspective/fixed inspection renderers are not covered.

## FMB1 format

Big-endian, every field word aligned:

| Offset | Size | Field |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 4 | `FMB1` |
| 4 | 2 | families F, 1..48 |
| 6 | 2 | frame slots N, 1..1024 |
| 8 | 48 | jobs 94..141 -> family, 255 none |
| 56 | 4·F | per family its box: rows, row bytes, rows above the feet, columns left of them |
| 56+4F | 40·F | per family 10 animations (pose·2 + back): first slot u16, count u8, loop ticks u8 |
| … | 2·(N+1) | slot offsets from the bank start, the last one = bank size |
| … | N (+1 to stay even) | each slot's wait, in ticks, 1..255 |
| … | | the frame pictures |

A frame: rows h (1..40), row bytes (2, 4 or 6: 16/32/48 columns), rows above
the feet (1..40, at most 12 below), columns left of the feet; then four planes
of h x row-bytes: light, dark, mask (1 = transparent), interior. Inside a plane
the 16-column strips come one after the other, each a run of h 16-bit rows, so
the renderer can draw a strip in place.

The family box holds every picture of every one of its poses, feet to feet (the
validator checks it). It is only a probe window, so it may be up to 64 columns
wide where a picture stops at 48: the source shifts a body sideways between
poses. The renderer reads what stands in front of that box once per place
instead of once per picture.

Every slot carries its own wait, because the original repeats pictures inside a
sequence at different rhythms (the idle is frames 11 10 9 10 11 12 13 12 for
6 8 10 8 ticks, twice). Slots showing the same picture share its bytes, and
identical whole sequences share their slots: for the four Chapter I families
that turns 239 slots into 99 pictures and 385 KB into 32 KB. Offsets are
therefore not chained; the cold validator checks every slot from its own
header, that each wait is at least one tick, that an animation's ticks add up
to its loop field, and that the pictures end exactly at the bank size.

The animation clock is the game tick (about 30 per second).

## The art: extracted from the disc, one command

```sh
make -C games/fft/campaign data/ffmb.89y          # the chapter's families
tools/pyenv/bin/python games/fft/tools/monster_body_assets.py \
    --families 94,97,100,104 -o games/fft/campaign/data/ffmb.bin --preview x/monster-body/sheet.png
```

`monster_body_assets.py` replays each selected family's retail body timelines
with the bounded oracle, converts every displayed frame at the family's
approved scale and palette (`monster_appearance.py`, checked against the
approved FMR1 standing planes), writes them as four-grey PNGs with a manifest
and packs them. The source waits are halved, since the original counts 60 ticks
a second and the game about 30. A flying family hovers for its idle and uses
its flight for walking, so the bank keeps five poses. The approved previews
remain `monster_combat_review.py`'s; this writes the art the game draws.

`MONSTER_BODY_FAMILIES` picks the families. The default is the ten the 33
Chapter I encounters place -- Chocobo, Goblin, Bomb, Cuar, Pisco Demon,
Skeleton, Ahriman, Steel Hawk, Minitaurus and Dragon, listed by
`chapter_audit.py`'s own enumeration of the chapter's story battles (ENTD
0x180..0x191) and first-tier random formations. Ghoul is not among them: it
appears in eleven encounters elsewhere in the game, none of them Chapter I.

The chapter ships **all sixteen appearances**, not only the ten its own
encounters place: the player may breed any family and deploy it. They are
135,566 bytes, past one archive variable, so the extractor splits them in order
and the loader reads **`ffmb`, `ffmc` then `ffmd`** (63,786 + 60,904 + 10,876).
A job belongs to exactly one bank and `monster_body_of` picks it.

Ahriman and Steel Hawk are flying sheets: their idle is the original hover and
their walk its flight, which is what the pictures show.

## Packing poses from your own pictures

```sh
tools/pyenv/bin/python games/fft/tools/monster_body_bank.py MANIFEST.json \
    -o games/fft/campaign/data/ffmb.bin --preview x/monster-body/sheet.png
```

The manifest lists families; each has its monster job IDs and, per pose
(`idle walk attack hurt dead`), `front` and `back` PNG lists, the feet
`foot: [x, y]` in the PNGs (per pose or per family) and either `wait` (the same
for every frame) or `waits` (one per frame, or one list per facing):

```json
{"families": [{"jobs": [94, 95, 96], "foot": [24, 39],
  "anims": {"idle": {"wait": 6, "front": ["a0.png", "a1.png"], "back": ["b0.png", "b1.png"]},
            "walk": {...}, "attack": {...}, "hurt": {...}, "dead": {...}}}]}
```

PNGs: four greys (255, 170, 85, 0) on transparency (alpha < 128), already with
their black edge; the packer adds the one-pixel white outline, crops, pools
repeated pictures and rejects a body beyond 48x40 or feet outside it.
`--preview` writes every distinct picture on light grey for a visual pass.
ROM-derived PNGs, manifests and banks stay local (ignored `x/` or `data/`),
never committed.

## What the bank holds, and whether anything can go

```sh
tools/pyenv/bin/python games/fft/tools/monster_body_review.py --bank data/ffmb.bin \
    --names x/monster-body/poses/families.json --out x/monster-body/review
```

`monster_body_review.py` decodes the packed bank, so it shows what the game
draws: `pictures.png` (every picture of every pose, front then back, with its
wait in ticks), `animations-N.gif` (each pose at the bank's own rhythm, the
original facing and its mirror) and `review.json`.

The Chapter I bank holds **99 pictures in 240 slots, 31,484 bytes of pictures
and 936 of tables**. Where they go, and what dropping a pose would really save:

| Pose | pictures | bytes | bytes used by no other pose |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Idle | 40 | 12,520 | 0 |
| Walk | 40 | 12,520 | 0 |
| Attack | 52 | 17,808 | 15,280 |
| Hurt | 8 | 2,704 | 1,992 |
| Dead | 7 | 980 | 980 |

Idle and walk cost nothing against each other: the original plays the same
eight pictures at two rhythms (6 8 10 8 ticks standing, 2 4 4 2 walking), and
the bank stores them once. The attack is the only large block, and it is the
only pose whose pictures nothing else uses.

Dropping pictures that merely look alike is not a lever here. Placed on their
feet and compared pixel by pixel, the closest two of the 99 differ by 8 pixels
and the median picture differs from its nearest neighbour by 111 -- about a
seventh of a body. Allowing a 4-pixel difference drops none; 8 pixels drops
one (324 bytes); even 64 pixels, which would visibly change the animation,
drops eight (2,072 bytes of 31,484). The shrink to four greys has not made the
source frames collide; pooling the exact repeats already took 385 KB to 32 KB.

## Verification

```sh
make -C games/fft/campaign campaign-monster-body-check
tools/pyenv/bin/python games/fft/campaign/check_monster_body.py --native
```

PC and ASan/UBSan agree (31 checks, `93DECFE8`); the 68000 run under
`ti-cycles` returns the same checks and 19 screen hashes. The idle screens of
both creatures in the four directions equal the PNGs composed independently by
the script; mirrored back views equal the flipped back views; nothing is drawn
below the water line; the diamond stays above the body. Also checked: malformed
banks rejected, job without a family, human job unchanged with or without the
bank, actor cache keys and misses untouched by a monster, idle/walk/attack/hurt/
Dead selection, attack held on its last frame, cover by real scenery found on
the map, left/right screen clipping, archive pointer reacquired on reentry.

## CPU of a full board

`check_capacity_render.py --case monster-body` puts wide bodies on the thirteen
actors of the private descriptor and replays 90 frames of the Gariland board
under `ti-cycles`; `--bodies N` dresses only the last N, `--body-scale` shrinks
the synthetic creatures. Ordinary frames, all thirteen actors wearing a body:

| Body size | first engine | strips/probe/in place | + remembered span |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 32x26, the bounds of the selected art | 834,948 / 947,338 | 521,738 / 629,698 | 349,259 / 490,348 |
| 48x40, the worst-case test shapes | 1,402,592 / 1,522,714 | 780,352 / 892,044 | 490,536 / 650,458 |

(average / peak, with the synthetic test creatures.) With the real Chapter I
bank (`--body-chapter`, one job per family the banks draw), thirteen bodies
cost 395,033 / 488,982 at that stage. Reading the scenery per family box and
keeping its bytes (below) brings the ten Chapter I families to **346,342 /
460,906** for thirteen bodies and 306,953 / 420,478 for six.

Only nine of the 89 ordinary frames are still over 360k, and they are frames
1 to 9: the opening, while the banner runs and every unit's cover is read for
the first time. The board's own first frames ramp the same way without a single
monster. From frame 10 the median is 340,396 and nothing exceeds the budget.

The native check harness no longer fits 64 KB with thirteen bodies and two
banks, so `check_capacity_render.py` builds it with `FFT_CHECK_NO_ZONES`: the
per-frame values the gate reads come from `FRAME_END`, not from the zone
markers, and only the per-zone breakdown is lost.

Four changes got it there, all of them pixel for pixel identical: PC,
ASan/UBSan and the 68000 keep the 31 checks, the same 19 screens and the same
board checksums 0546/ECF7/A87E/AA26 at every body count.

1. **Strip layout.** Inside an FMB1 plane the 16-column strips are contiguous,
   so a strip can be read as a run of 16-bit rows.
2. **One probe per row.** The scenery in front is read once per row for every
   strip at once, instead of once per strip (`body_cover_row`); the view's
   depth base and stride are taken once per body, since `SC_H` is a runtime
   value in a chapter and naming `VDEPTH`/`DEPTH_STRIDE` costs a multiply.
3. **Drawn in place.** On the 68000 a strip that nothing changes -- no mirror,
   flash, water or scenery in front -- is handed to `draw_sprite` straight from
   the archive, as the human sprite already was; only the rows the scenery
   really bites are rebuilt.
4. **The scenery read per family box, and kept.** Probing what stands in front
   of a body costs about 1,235 cycles a row, and a body changes picture every
   three to five ticks, so a probe keyed on the picture was read again three or
   four times a frame. The family box is the same for every pose, so `Cover`
   now keeps, per unit, the place, depth and orientation it probed, the box
   rows the scenery stands in front of, and those rows' bytes
   (`bx/by/bd/brot/b0/b1/bvalid/bc`, 266 bytes a unit, 3,320 bytes of `Work`).
   A new picture of the same animation then reads nothing at all: over 90
   frames the probe runs six times instead of 540. The row loop also lost its
   per-body `memset`, keeps each strip's interior plane in a register and reads
   a plain word inline instead of calling out for it.

Note for anyone measuring this: `MOD_SIG` contains `sizeof(Work)`, so a change
to `Work` makes every prebuilt module bank stale and the battle then refuses to
start with no memory message at all (`vbuf`/`sbuf` null, the body checks fall
to two). Rebuild `data/ffbm.89y data/ffbn.89y data/ffbo.89y` first, and read
that build's output: a module compile error leaves the old banks in place and
`make` reports nothing wrong. Any constant in `Work`'s layout must be defined
where both the core and a module see it, not behind `MOD_CORE`. `Work` is now
33,400 bytes, 3,320 more than before this cache; scene + `Work` + 2 still
leaves 15,958 bytes under the 65,520-byte AMS block ceiling, but the installed
16KiB/peak certification moves that much further away.

Size: the resident TTPack input grows from 61,491 to 63,481 bytes (headroom
3,445 → 1,455; zone 8,702 unchanged): about 490 bytes of validator, 1,500 of
rendering. Moving the cold validator into the RESOURCES image would return the
490 bytes if the margin is needed. The FKO1 check's `sizeof(Work)` assertion
(29,904 on PC) already fails on the preceding `main`; this change adds no field.

## What the monsters cost in all

| Bank | Bytes | What it holds |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `ffmb` | 63,786 | battle bodies: 8 appearances, 24 jobs, 5 poses |
| `ffmc` | 60,904 | seven more appearances, 21 jobs |
| `ffmd` | 10,876 | the sixteenth |
| `ffma` | 13,082 | formation and Status: 16 appearances and portraits, 48 job aliases |
| `ffeg` | 640 | the eggs (4 pictures, 8 variants) |
| `ffwb` | 285 | the breeding rules |
| **total** | **149,573** | what an install carries for monsters |

These banks are the same for every chapter, so they belong to the core package
rather than to each chapter's; moving them there is the packaging step, not
done here.
The appearance policy is unchanged: one body per family of three jobs, no
palette-only copy -- Chocobo/Black Chocobo/Red Chocobo, Goblin/Black
Goblin/Gobbledeguck, Bomb/Grenade/Explosive and Red Panther/Cuar/Vampire each
share one appearance while keeping their own names, stats and save identities.

Banks packed before this layout change must be packed again (the frame bytes
moved, and the slot waits and family boxes are new). `Work` grew by 208
bytes, so `MOD_SIG` changed: the module banks and the shipping core package
must be rebuilt and the chapter packages matched again.
