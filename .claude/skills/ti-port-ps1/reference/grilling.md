# Grilling a PS1 study

Run `/grilling` after `SKILL.md` §1 (the disc read, the game booted to play, screenshots of
candidate places) and before any study. Ask about decisions, never about facts the tools can
find (which room has three heights is found by playing the game headless, not asked). Every
question carries a recommended default; questions are technical and specific to the mechanic.
Challenge any answer that grows the scope and propose the smallest useful alternative.
Record the answers in `RE_NOTES.md` § Decisions, give the user a short summary, go on.

## Round 1: what the experiment is

1. **The goal and the stop line.** Default: one standalone room with the mechanic playable
   (Alundra: move, jump, several heights, ledges, walls, readable depth); no combat, enemies,
   original assets or original rooms. Done = the success sentence the user agrees to.
2. **Fidelity.** Which aspects of the original matter: trajectory, timing, ledge rules,
   visual elevation, responsiveness, or all? Default: the *feel* (timing and reach in
   seconds and tiles) and the *rules* (what is reachable), not the numbers or the internals.
3. **The reference situation.** Which place in the original to measure on (offer the
   screenshots). Default: the first place with free control and at least two heights.

## Round 2: the rules of the target (Alundra-style traversal)

4. **Jump trigger.** A button only, or also automatic hops onto small steps? Default: button
   only (one rule, testable); auto-hop only if the original does it and the user wants it.
5. **Heights.** Discrete levels (how many: default 3, 0-2, maybe 4) or continuous?
   Default: discrete; 1 level = 8 rendered pixels on screen (a 16-pixel step eats too much of
   100 rows; 4 is hard to read).
6. **Reachable ledge.** Default: a jump clears +1 level, never +2 (an unreachable ledge then
   behaves as a wall); walking up +1 without jumping is blocked.
7. **Dropping.** Walk off any height (default: yes, a fall at the jump's descent speed, no
   damage) or only jump down? Special behaviour for big falls? Default: none.
8. **Slopes and stairs.** Default: none in the first room (stairs = a tile that changes level
   by walking, a later milestone if wanted).
9. **Diagonals and air control.** 8 directions (default yes: the TI arrows allow two at
   once), steering during a jump (default: as the original measures).
10. **Collision resolution.** Tile (16×16) or sub-tile: a footprint box smaller than a tile
    (default: an 8×6 or 10×8 foot box against per-tile heights, so narrow passages and corners
    feel right without per-pixel shapes).

## Round 3: presentation and target

11. **Sizes.** 16×16 tiles (default yes); the player drawn 12-14 × 16 inside a ~16 footprint
    (default), white outline (visibility rule).
12. **The view.** One room on one screen (160×100 = 10 × 6.25 tiles) or scrolling? Default:
    a room slightly larger than the screen with a simple camera only if the test room needs
    it; otherwise one screen (10 × 6 tiles plus the HUD/debug line).
13. **Controls.** Arrows to move; jump on [2nd] (`K_A`), other buttons free (default);
    ESC quits; debug overlay toggled by a key.
14. **Depth.** Default: tiles drawn row by row from back to front with the player inserted at
    its row by (y, z); raised terrain shows a front face (a darker band of the level height)
    and the player's shadow stays on the ground under a jump.
15. **Art source.** The disc's own graphics (tiles and sprites from the VRAM, converted to
    4 greys, fitted to the target sizes; kept local, regenerated by a build tool) or our own
    drawing / placeholders. Ask it even when the engine is "not a port": Alundra's user wanted
    the disc's art. Default: placeholders until the presentation milestone, then the disc's.
16. **Toolchain.** Default: C on the Portable Game Runtime (`runtime/`), PC first, TI last,
    grayscale; no asm.

## Round for a view or rendering study (FFT's Gariland; adapt)

17. **The map.** The game's own level data (extracted at build time, local) or a hand-made
    one inspired by it (committed, builds without the disc)? Default: the game's, when its
    format is documented and small.
18. **Scale.** Answer with mock-ups: three tile sizes rendered on the real map with units at
    scale, the screen outlined on the whole map; the user picks (FFT: 24 x 12, 1 h = 6 px).
19. **Rotation / view changes.** Animated (polygon frames between the views, ~0.5 s) or
    instantaneous? Default: animated, a few frames, with the composed view at the end.
20. **Occlusion.** FFT's way (turn the camera to see behind) or fading roofs? Default: the
    game's way; prove it with a count of the hidden unit's pixels per view.

## Milestones (Alundra; adapt per game)

1. Behaviour: `RE_NOTES.md` § Behaviour and § Numbers for movement, jump, heights, ledges,
   landing, collision with elevated terrain.
2. Simulation on flat ground: a rectangle that moves and jumps (z, the jump table, landing),
   debug overlay, unit tests.
3. Height map: discrete heights per tile, drawn as numbers and shades.
4. Ledges: jump up +1, blocked at +2, jump/walk down.
5. Collision: walls, corners, obstacles, during ascent and descent; the test room complete.
6. 16×16 presentation: tiles, the player sprite with outline, the shadow, depth order.
7. TI-oriented: data sizes, `ti-cycles` per frame, `xcheck`, the emulator once.
8. (Optional) an approximation of an original room.
